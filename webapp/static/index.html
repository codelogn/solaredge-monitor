vs Peers is this optimizer's average power over all its readings in a 15-minute window, as a % of the median of its peers in the same window (whole array) — so sun angle and cloud cancel out, and 100% means "producing like its neighbours". Every reading counts, including near-zero ones: checked against the inverter's own DC power, those are real. Score multiplies output-vs-peers by reporting reliability, so neither problem can mask the other. Worst first.

☀ columns are daylight-only — including night would drag every figure toward zero. They show median rather than minimum: every panel genuinely touches ~0 A at some point, so the minimum can't tell panels apart. Near 0 is the share of daylight readings under 0.2 A. It tends to be higher on weaker or shaded panels, but only loosely — healthy panels can sit there 15–20% of the time — so treat it as supporting evidence next to vs Peers and the hourly profile, not a verdict.
Always check Confidence before acting — a verdict from a handful of samples is noise. Low output can also mean shade from a tree or chimney rather than a fault, so treat this as a shortlist to investigate, not a diagnosis.

Data sources & reliability — which to trust, and when

Inverter
over your LAN (Modbus TCP)		 SolarEdge server
over the internet
What it gives The whole inverter: DC and AC power, voltage, current, frequency, status, heatsink temperature, lifetime energy counter. Each optimizer: panel voltage, optimizer output voltage, current, power, interval energy — plus the site layout (serials, positions, panel models).
How it gets here This server asks the inverter directly, every 30 seconds. No internet involved. Optimizers → inverter (over the DC power lines) → the inverter's internet connection → SolarEdge's servers → this server, polled every 2 minutes. The SolarEdge app shows this same data.
How current Real time. Normally ~2 minutes old. Hours old when the inverter's uploads fall behind.
How it fails A read fails outright (counted in Modbus Reads), or the inverter answers with an old snapshot, which is detected and discarded. Energy totals stay correct across gaps, because the inverter's own counter keeps counting. Data arrives late, or a stretch never arrives. Nothing that arrives is wrong — each reading carries the time it was measured — but late or missing stretches leave holes in the per-panel history.
Per-panel detail None — the inverter only reports its totals. The only source for individual panels and optimizers.
Trust it for Is it producing right now, today's energy and peak, inverter status and faults, and anything that should match your utility bill. Which panel or optimizer is weak, shade patterns by hour, reporting problems of a single optimizer.

Should I worry about SolarEdge data when the upload is behind? Not about its accuracy — about its completeness and freshness. Late readings are still correct and are analysed at the time they were measured, so verdicts stay valid. But while it's behind, the Optimizers table shows the panels as they were hours ago, and stretches that never arrive can't be scored — typically afternoons, if the link degrades as the day goes on. The Per-panel data flag at the top of the page and the Cloud Delay card say when this is happening.

Is Modbus data more reliable? For whole-system figures, yes: it comes straight from the inverter, in real time, with no cloud in between, and its energy counter is the same one SolarEdge eventually reports. When the two disagree, believe the inverter. But it can't tell you anything about individual panels, and if reads are failing, the "now" figures can be minutes old — see the Inverter data flag.

Why doesn't the dashboard match the SolarEdge app? The app shows SolarEdge server data, so it carries the same upload delay. Inverter-tagged figures here are live; the app catches up once the inverter's backlog uploads.

Both flags red at once? Modbus and the cloud upload usually share the inverter's network connection. Failing Modbus reads together with a growing cloud delay point at that connection — a weak Wi-Fi link is the usual cause, and a wired Ethernet connection to the inverter is the usual fix. Neither affects how much the panels produce.
